Sui Bridge.

A Sui Bridge moves ETH, USDC and other assets from Ethereum and other connected chains onto Sui, a Move-based layer-1 built around an object model rather than account balances — the reason bridged tokens arrive as distinct on-chain objects instead of ledger entries. You pick the asset and amount, review the fee and the amount that will arrive before you sign, pay the source-chain gas, and your funds stay in your own wallet the entire time.

How the estimate is calculated

Fee = 0.25 units + 0.3% of the amount, rounded to two decimal places and capped at the amount sent. The remaining amount uses the same units, at a 1:1 conversion. Time starts at 2 minutes, adds 1 minute per 2,500 units or part thereof, and is capped at 12 minutes. The displayed range adds 2 minutes to that estimate.

What a Sui Bridge does

A Sui Bridge locks or burns your asset on the source chain and mints the matching representation on Sui, so value moves onto the network without any asset ever leaving self-custody. When you deposit, a bridge contract on Ethereum, or another connected chain, receives your asset and Sui credits the equivalent to your address once the deposit is finalized. When you withdraw, the reverse happens: the asset is burned on Sui and released on the source chain after the bridge's validators or guardians confirm the exit. The amount that will arrive, the fee and the estimated time are shown before you sign, so there is no guesswork at the moment of approval. This locking-and-minting pattern is what every credible bridge uses, whether the destination is a rollup or a standalone layer-1 like Sui, and it is why a canonical route never needs to hold your assets beyond the moment a deposit or withdrawal is actually in flight.

Wormhole-based routes vs the native Sui Bridge

Most assets reach Sui through the Wormhole docs-documented guardian network, which watches the source chain and attests that a deposit happened before Sui mints the wrapped token. Sui also runs its own native bridge for a smaller set of core assets such as ETH, secured by a dedicated validator committee instead of Wormhole's guardians. Both routes count as a Sui Bridge in everyday use; they differ in who attests to the deposit and which assets each one supports.

What you can bring across

ETH, USDC and a growing list of ERC-20 tokens are the common deposits, and the same mechanics apply whether the source is Ethereum or another chain Wormhole connects. Once an asset lands on Sui it exists as an object owned by your address rather than a line in a shared balance table, which is part of what lets the network process transfers of different assets without queuing them through one global state.

Wrapped vs native USDC on Sui

Not every USDC on Sui is the same token. Circle issues native USDC directly on Sui through its own minting process, while a Sui Bridge route can also deliver a wrapped representation of USDC that originated on Ethereum. Both spend inside Sui apps, but they are technically different objects until a bridge or Circle's own transfer mechanism unifies them, so check which one a given app expects before you deposit.

Why two USDCs exist

Native USDC is minted and burned by Circle's own contracts on Sui, with no bridge involved and no wrapped-asset risk. Bridged USDC, by contrast, is a wrapped token: it is backed one-to-one by USDC locked on Ethereum and redeemable through the same Sui Bridge route that created it. Liquidity for each can differ across Sui's decentralized exchanges, so the better choice depends on where you plan to use it.

How to tell them apart

Check the token's contract address on the Sui explorer rather than trusting a symbol alone — wrapped and native USDC can show the same ticker in a wallet while carrying different object types on-chain. Confirm the address a destination app lists before you bridge, so the token you deliver is the one it actually accepts.

SUI: the gas token you need after a Sui Bridge

Sui does not use ETH for gas. Every transaction on the network, including moving the assets you just bridged, is paid in SUI, so the first thing to plan for after a Sui Bridge deposit is a small SUI balance. Many people bridge a little SUI alongside their main asset; others arrive with ETH or USDC and swap a small amount for SUI on a Sui decentralized exchange before doing anything else. Either way, treat SUI the way Sui docs recommend treating gas on any chain: keep enough for several transactions, not just one, so a swap or a withdrawal never fails for want of fee money. Gas on Sui also covers the storage your new objects occupy, and part of what you pay is refunded as a storage rebate when an object is later deleted, which is a detail worth knowing if your balance after a transaction looks slightly different from what a simple gas estimate predicted.

Sui Bridge fees and timing

A Sui Bridge charges you the source-chain gas to run the deposit transaction plus any small protocol fee; once your funds are on Sui, activity there is paid in SUI and costs very little. Timing depends on the route and direction — deposits confirm once the source chain finalizes, while withdrawals wait for the bridge's validators or guardians to attest the exit. Judge a route by the net amount

Compare any bridge route by the net amount received after gas and fees, not the headline rate. For small transfers the fixed source-chain gas can dominate, so bridging a tiny amount is rarely worth it; batching a larger deposit spreads that cost across more value. You can confirm the amount that actually landed by checking your address on the Sui explorer afterward.

Deposit and withdrawal flow, step by step

The flow is the same every time, which is what makes a Sui Bridge predictable once you have done it once.
  • Connect your self-custody wallet and choose Ethereum, or another supported chain, as the source and Sui as the destination.
  • Enter the amount; the route shows the fee and the amount that will arrive.
  • Approve the token if it is an ERC-20, then sign the deposit transaction and pay source-chain gas.
  • Wait for source-chain finality — your balance appears on Sui as a new object a few minutes later.
  • To exit, start a withdrawal on Sui, wait for the bridge's attestation, then claim on the source chain.

Why the object model changes what you see on Sui

Sui tracks assets as individual objects owned by an address instead of balances in a shared table. After a Sui Bridge deposit, the token you receive is a distinct object you can inspect, split or merge, and an app that wants to move it must be explicitly given access to that object rather than a generic balance check. This is why some Sui wallets show bridged tokens as separate entries even when they share the same coin type, and it is normal.

Is a Sui Bridge safe to use?

A canonical Sui Bridge keeps your keys in your wallet and never takes custody, so the main risks are the ones you control at signing. Confirm the destination token's object type, check the amount received against the amount you sent, and review the approval you are granting on the source chain. Bookmark the official bridge interface rather than searching for it each time, since a lookalike site is one of the more common ways people end up signing something other than what they intended.

Approvals and allowances

Prefer a limited approval over an unlimited one so a contract can only ever move the amount you intend, and revoke old allowances you no longer use on Ethereum. The fewer standing approvals your wallet carries, the smaller the surface any single risk can touch.

The one rule that never changes

No bridge, wallet or support agent ever needs your seed phrase. Anyone asking for it is attempting fraud. A real Sui Bridge only ever asks you to sign a transaction you can read in your wallet — it never asks you to type or paste your recovery words anywhere.

Sui Bridge vs other routes onto Sui

You can reach Sui through the native Sui Bridge, a Wormhole-based route, or a third-party liquidity bridge. The native and Wormhole routes mint the official representation of your asset and are the reference path for large or long-term transfers. A third-party bridge can be faster for withdrawals because it fronts liquidity rather than waiting for attestation, but it adds its own fee and its own trust assumptions. For most users the rule is simple: use the route described in Sui's own documentation and check deposits flowing onto the network on DefiLlama unless you specifically need a faster exit and have checked the third party's reputation and the net amount it quotes. If you are coming from Ethereum for the first time, ethereum.org keeps a general primer on how bridging out of Ethereum works before you pick a specific route. Whichever path you choose, the destination object type and the fee quote are the two things worth re-checking right before you sign, since those are the details a rushed transfer is most likely to get wrong.
What makes something a Sui Bridge rather than a regular swap?
A Sui Bridge locks or burns your asset on the source chain and mints the matching representation on Sui, moving value between networks without a central party taking custody. A swap, by contrast, exchanges one asset for another on the same chain.
Do I need SUI after a Sui Bridge deposit?
Yes. Gas on Sui is paid in SUI, not ETH, so keep a small SUI balance to cover your first transactions. You can bridge a little SUI with your main asset or swap for it on a Sui decentralized exchange after you arrive.
What's the difference between wrapped and native USDC on Sui?
Native USDC is minted directly by Circle on Sui with no bridge involved. Wrapped USDC arrives through a Sui Bridge route and is backed by USDC locked on Ethereum. Both spend in Sui apps, but they are different objects, so check which one a given app expects.
Why is a Sui withdrawal sometimes slower than a deposit?
Deposits credit on Sui once the source chain finalizes, which can take only minutes. Withdrawals wait for the bridge's validators or guardians to attest that the exit is valid before releasing funds on the source chain, which can take longer depending on the route.
How much does a Sui Bridge transfer cost?
You pay source-chain gas plus any small protocol fee to deposit; activity on Sui itself is paid in SUI and costs very little. Compare routes by the net amount received after gas and fees rather than the headline figure.
Do I need an account to use a Sui Bridge?
No. You connect a self-custody wallet and bridge on-chain, with no sign-up and no custody. Your funds stay under your own keys the whole time.
What happens if my deposit fails?
A failed transaction reverts and you keep your assets; only the source-chain gas spent on the attempt is lost. Nothing is credited on Sui until the deposit is confirmed and finalized.
